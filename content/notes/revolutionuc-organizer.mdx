## The other side of the table

At RevolutionUC 2025 I was on the hacker side: a team, a deadline and a project named after the hackathon's mascot. A few months later I was one of the people making the event happen. Organizing gave me a completely different perspective on the same weekend. As a hacker you see your project. As an organizer you see the registrations, the sponsors, the judges, the schedule, and the website that holds it all together.

## Rebuilding the website

The Web Team rebuilt the site on a new stack, Next.js and TypeScript, and I wrote much of the front end: 54 of the repository's 93 commits.

The 2026 site has a travel theme, so most of my work was about making it feel like taking off:

- **The hero.** Clouds part as you scroll, each one sliding sideways and growing on its own ScrollTrigger, and a plane flies up and out of the hero as you leave it. The event dates sit up front.
- **Motion throughout:** smooth scrolling with ScrollSmoother, with the clouds tuned to move with it, and SplitText reveals.
- **The sections people actually read:** About, prizes, tracks and the FAQ, updated from the previous year.
- **Forms for everyone who isn't a hacker.** Sponsors and judges or mentors got their own interest forms, and later I improved the registration form itself, from the phone number input to the age field.
- **Phones.** The hero, the clouds, the plane and the tracks each got a dedicated mobile layout.

Not every animation idea survived. A plane went in, came out and went back in. And as the site grew more pages, GSAP's pinning started breaking navigation between them, until a teammate untangled it by dropping the pins and cleaning up every trigger before a page unmounts. It was the same lesson I ran into later when I rebuilt this portfolio: an animation has to know how to leave, not just how to arrive.

Beyond the public site, I also created an internal admin dashboard with Next.js and REST APIs to streamline organizer workflows.

## Working across teams

The website touches almost every part of a hackathon, so I coordinated with five departments to get what each of them needed onto the site.

That changed how I think about leadership. It isn't about controlling decisions. It's about facilitating collaboration and valuing the ideas each team brings.

## What it gave me

Organizing strengthened my leadership, communication and collaboration skills in a way no project had. At the end of the academic year I received the 2026 Top Students Active in ACM/IEEE & Annual Hackathon Organization Award from UC's Department of Computer Science.
